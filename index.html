Your review checklist

GUIDANCE
01 · Confirm the services

Compare each line with the services you received. Check dates, quantities, and unfamiliar codes. Ask the provider to explain anything you do not recognise.

02 · Check the total

Review the itemized charges and make sure the listed amounts add up to the stated total. Ask about repeated entries, fees, and adjustments.

03 · Compare insurance information

If insurance applies, compare the bill with the Explanation of Benefits. Check the deductible, copayment, coinsurance, and any amount the plan says it did not cover.

04 · Ask for written clarification

Request an itemized statement and an explanation of any disputed line. Keep copies of messages and notes from calls.

These are review prompts, not confirmed billing errors. No savings estimate is calculated.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check when a medical bill looks wrong?

Start with an itemized statement. Check the patient and service dates, whether each service was received, the quantities billed, and any repeated or unfamiliar line items. Compare the bill with your insurer’s Explanation of Benefits (EOB), if you have one. A similar-looking charge is not automatically an error, so ask the billing office to explain it before drawing a conclusion.

What can I do if my insurance claim is denied?

Read the denial notice carefully and note the reason, the appeal deadline, and any documents requested. Ask your insurer to identify the plan provision used for the decision and explain how to submit an appeal. Gather relevant records, referral or authorisation details, receipts, and your EOB. Follow the instructions and keep copies of everything you send. Your insurer or healthcare provider can confirm the process that applies to your plan.

Can I ask a provider to review or correct a bill?

Yes. Contact the billing department, explain the specific line item you want reviewed, and request an itemized statement and written explanation. If you believe an insurance payment or adjustment is missing, ask the provider to recheck the claim with your insurer. Keep a record of the date, the person you spoke with, and any reference number. Ask whether the payment due date can be paused while the issue is reviewed; do not assume it has been paused unless they confirm it.

Can MedSave guarantee that I will save money?

No. A service cannot responsibly guarantee that every bill contains an error or that a provider or insurer will change a charge. The amount you owe depends on the services received, provider billing, your insurance benefits, and your plan terms. Careful checking can help you ask informed questions, but any correction or savings must be confirmed by the provider or insurer.

How is my medical information handled?

In this website version, choosing a file displays it in your browser; the file is not sent to a server for analysis. Avoid using real medical documents in a public demo. Before any service accepts health records online, it should clearly explain how information is collected, protected, retained, and deleted, and meet the privacy and security requirements that apply where it operates.

Does MedSave replace my insurer, provider, or a qualified adviser?

No. MedSave is intended to help you organise information and prepare questions. Your provider can explain the services billed, your insurer can confirm coverage and appeal rules, and a qualified professional can advise on individual legal or medical questions. Verify important deadlines and decisions directly with the organisation responsible.

Does the website actually read my uploaded bill?

No. This version lets you select a file in your browser, but it does not extract the document's text or perform automated bill analysis. The checklist is general guidance. A live analysis feature would need to be built and tested separately before it could make claims about a particular bill.
